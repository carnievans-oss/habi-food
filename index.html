Two services, one mission

Rescue & rehabilitation are not the same thing.

Most people use the terms interchangeably. They shouldn't. Our rescue service is the field response — finding, containing, and safely transporting injured wildlife. Our rehabilitation service is the specialist work that follows: medical care, nutrition, recovery housing, and the long road back to the wild.

  • Rescue is fastMinutes matter. Field response, expert containment, vet referral and intake.
  • Rehab is slowDays to months of species-specific care, with release readiness as the only acceptable outcome.
  • Both require expertiseDone badly, either step kills more animals than it saves. We don't cut corners.

About us

An independent voice for wildlife in Victoria.

Totally Untamed Wildlife Rescue & Rehabilitation is a privately operated rescue and rehabilitation service based in Hampton Park. Founded and run by Carni Evans, we exist because native wildlife in this region needed someone to step up — not wait for a fragmented system to catch up.

📌

Rescue vs rehabilitation — an important distinction

Our rescue service responds to calls about injured or displaced native wildlife in the field. Our rehabilitation service is the specialist care that follows — providing the environment, nutrition and medical support for animals to recover and return to the wild. These are two separate, complementary operations.

The motivation

Because no one was doing it quite like this.

Australia has the highest mammal extinction rate in the world. Victoria's wildlife is under pressure from habitat loss, urban expansion, vehicle strikes, domestic cats and dogs, and climate stress. Official systems exist — but they're fragmented, under-resourced, and difficult for the public to navigate.

⚠️

The coordination gap is real

Every day, injured animals are missed, mishandled or lost to the system because information doesn't flow between organisations, volunteers, carers and the public. We built Totally Untamed to address that gap directly — not to wait for someone else to do it.

We do this because we believe knowledge and action are both needed. It's not enough to care — you have to show up, adapt, and keep going when the system fails you. That's the Totally Untamed way.

Emergency guidance

Found an injured native animal? Read this first.

Stay calm. Don't rush. The right steps in the first five minutes make a real difference to that animal's chance of survival.

📞

First call: Wildlife Victoria

1300 094 535 — 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. They will advise you on safe handling and connect you to a local carer.

1

Assess from a distance

Don't rush in. Watch the animal for 30–60 seconds. Is it moving? Is it alert? Is there a risk to you (traffic, dog, etc)?

2

Make the area safe

Remove threats where possible. Keep pets and bystanders back. If on a road, only assist if it's safe to do so.

3

Call for expert advice

Call Wildlife Victoria (1300 094 535) BEFORE touching the animal if possible. They'll guide you on safe handling for that species.

4

Contain carefully if needed

A dark, ventilated box lined with a soft towel works for most small animals. Warmth is critical for joeys and small birds.

5

Do not feed or give water

Well-intentioned feeding is one of the most common causes of secondary injury and death in wildlife. Wait for guidance.

6

Transport to a vet or carer

Any vet in Victoria is legally obligated to provide free initial treatment to injured native wildlife. Use them when needed.

Critical guidance

The five most common mistakes.

Things people do with the best intentions that actually hurt animals — and what to do instead.

❌ Don't feed the animal

Bread, milk, pet food, fruit and even water can be harmful or fatal to native wildlife. Wait for expert guidance.

✅ Do call first

Ring Wildlife Victoria (1300 094 535) before handling. 60 seconds on the phone could save an animal's life.

❌ Don't over-handle

Stress kills. Minimise handling, reduce noise and light. Keep the animal warm and dark until a carer arrives.

✅ Do keep it warm and dark

A box lined with a soft towel, in a warm quiet spot, is the right first step for almost all small native animals.

❌ Don't release prematurely

An animal that appears recovered often isn't. Releasing too early is a leading cause of secondary death.

✅ Do check pouches

If you find a dead female kangaroo, wallaby or possum — always check the pouch. A live joey may be inside.

❌ Don't touch bats bare-handed

Flying foxes and microbats can carry Australian Bat Lyssavirus. Only vaccinated handlers should touch bats.

✅ Do use a vet

Any vet in Victoria treats native wildlife for free in an emergency. Use this if you can't reach a carer quickly.

Active initiatives

Beyond day-to-day rescue.

These initiatives create lasting change for wildlife habitat and care knowledge across Victoria.

🌿

HabiFood Program

Habitat food & corridor planting initiative

▾

HabiFood is a community-based native planting initiative focused on creating food and shelter corridors for local wildlife. By working with residents, councils and community groups to plant targeted native species, we're rebuilding fragmented habitat patch by patch. The program matches species to locations based on what wildlife actually needs — not just what looks nice in a garden.

Status: early pilot phase
📊

Rehabilitation Research Study

Data-driven care improvement

▾

A structured data collection study tracking outcomes, care protocols and case records across our rehabilitation work. The goal is to generate real-world evidence about what works in independent small-scale wildlife rehabilitation — and share that knowledge openly with the wider carer community.

Status: study design phase
🏠

Possum Box Mounting System

Original TUWRR design — full documentation

▾

We developed an original possum box mounting, installation and emergency removal system after identifying that standard approaches were unsafe, inconsistent and poorly documented. The TUWRR system includes a full SWMS (Safe Work Method Statement), risk assessment matrix, minimum equipment checklist, CFA/arborist directive signage, and emergency removal quick guide — all publicly available.

Status: published & in use

Original TUWRR design

The Possum Box Mounting System.

A complete, documented, safe-work system for installing and managing possum nest boxes. We built it because no one else had — and it's freely available.

1

SWMS — Safe Work Method Statement

Full safe work method statement for installation, routine inspection, release and emergency removal. Covers hazard identification, risk controls and emergency procedures.

2

Risk Assessment Matrix

Structured risk assessment covering all phases — from site selection through to decommission. Standard consequence/likelihood matrix format.

3

Minimum Equipment Checklist

The equipment required to safely install, inspect and remove possum boxes — including PPE, tools and containment materials.

4

Emergency Removal Quick Guide

A fast-reference guide for emergency box removal scenarios — designed for use in the field when speed and safety both matter.

5

CFA / Arborist Directive Signage

A5 signage for boxes in fire-risk areas, communicating the presence of wildlife boxes to CFA crews and arborists conducting emergency work.

6

Community Education Plan Set

Resources and signage for community engagement around the box system and wildlife in residential trees.
